# Code of Conduct

This code of conduct is adapted from the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/) and the [tidyverse Code of Conduct](https://dplyr.tidyverse.org/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.html). It applies to everyone who participates in this project, including the core team, contributors, and anyone who opens an issue or pull request.

## Our commitment to diversity and inclusion

We want this project to be a welcoming and harassment-free space for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation. We value the different backgrounds and perspectives that people bring, and we believe they make our work better.

## Expected behaviour

Everyone participating in this project is expected to:

- be respectful and considerate toward other people, including when you disagree with them;
- give and accept constructive feedback gracefully, focusing on the work rather than the person;
- assume good intentions and ask questions before drawing conclusions;
- take responsibility for your mistakes, apologize to those affected, and learn from them;
- credit other people's work and ideas; and
- focus on what is best for the project and the people working on it.

## Unacceptable behaviour

The following behaviour is not acceptable:

- harassment, intimidation, or discrimination in any form;
- insulting, demeaning, or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks;
- sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances;
- trolling or deliberately disrupting discussions;
- publishing other people's private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission; and
- any other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional or academic setting.

## Consequences of unacceptable behaviour

Depending on how serious the behaviour is and whether it is repeated, the project team may take any of the following actions:

1. **Correction:** a private, written conversation explaining why the behaviour was inappropriate, and a request to stop.
2. **Warning:** a formal warning with clear consequences for continued behaviour, which may include a period without interaction with the people involved.
3. **Temporary ban:** a temporary ban from contributing to or interacting with the project.
4. **Permanent ban:** a permanent ban from any interaction with the project.

For members of the core team, unacceptable behaviour will also be reported to the DSCI 522 teaching team and handled under the University of British Columbia's policies on respectful conduct.
